@ Chef Henry’s Cheesy Truffles | tv | 23
s: Makes 25-30 balls
t: Prep 30 min | Chill overnight | Cook 10 min
> Chef Henry Vigar shared this great recipe with me. I had my 40th birthday at his restaurant La Mouette in 2015. Perfect as a canapé, snack, or indulgent starter!
# For the truffle balls
- 1 litre milk
- 200 g butter
- 150 g cake flour
- 50 g cornflour
- 100 g grated Fontina cheese (or similar melting cheese)
- Truffle oil, to taste
- Salt, to taste
# For coating and frying
- Semolina (for rolling)
- Extra flour
- 1–2 eggs, beaten
- Breadcrumbs (panko recommended)
- Oil, for deep frying
. Bring the milk to a simmer in a saucepan. In another large saucepan, melt the butter, then add the cake flour and cornflour. Stir constantly and cook out for 1 minute without letting it colour.
. Gradually add the hot milk, whisking continuously. Bring to the boil and cook for 5 minutes, until the mixture thickens and starts to pull away from the sides of the pan.
. Remove from the heat and whisk in the grated Fontina cheese. Add a drizzle of truffle oil and season with salt to taste. Cover and refrigerate overnight until firm.
. Once chilled and set, roll the mixture into small balls (about the size of a walnut). Coat each ball by rolling it in semolina or flour, then dip in beaten egg, and coat in breadcrumbs.
. Heat oil to high temperature (about 180°C) and fry the balls until golden and crisp. Optional: give them a few seconds in a hot oven to finish heating through, if needed.
. Serve hot with a sprinkle of salt, or with a dipping sauce of your choice. Goes really well with Zannie’s Sweet Mustard Sauce (Page 48).

@ Moroccan Roast Carrot and Avocado Salad | tv | 25
s: 4
t: 50 min
> This is a delicious side dish to any winter stew, and is a real winner at dinner. Enjoy this vibrant, warm salad full of bold Moroccan flavours! We filmed this dish as part of Ilse Kook!, a cooking show on kykNET.
# For the Salad
- 500 g mixed-colour medium carrots (with leafy tops if available)
- 2 tsp cumin seeds
- 1–2 small dried chillies
- 1 tsp sea salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 2 garlic cloves, peeled
- 4 sprigs fresh thyme (leaves picked)
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tbsp white wine vinegar
- 1 orange, halved
- 1 lemon, halved
- 3 avocados
- Approx. 60 ml extra olive oil and vinegar (for dressing)
- 4 slices ciabatta, toasted and torn
- 200 g mixed winter leaves (e.g., rocket, radicchio, witlof)
- 2 punnets cress
- 150 ml fat-free natural yoghurt
- 4 tbsp toasted sesame seeds
. Preheat the oven to 180°C.
. Parboil the carrots in salted boiling water for 10 minutes, until nearly tender. Drain and transfer to a roasting tray.
. In a pestle and mortar, grind the cumin seeds, dried chillies, sea salt, and black pepper. Add garlic, thyme leaves, olive oil, and vinegar. Pound into a paste.
. Pour the paste over the carrots and toss to coat. Add the orange and lemon halves, cut-side down, to the tray. Roast in the oven for 25–30 minutes, or until golden.
. Halve, peel, and stone the avocados. Cut into wedges lengthwise and place in a large bowl. Add the roasted carrots and torn ciabatta to the bowl with the avocados.
. Squeeze the juice from the roasted orange and lemon into a bowl. Add any pan juices. Mix with equal parts olive oil and a splash of vinegar. Season to taste.
. Toss the roasted vegetables, avocado, ciabatta, mixed leaves, and cress with the dressing. Arrange on a serving platter or divide between plates.
. Top with a dollop of yoghurt and a sprinkle of toasted sesame seeds.

@ Tomato and Pancetta Braai Chicken with Olives | tv | 26
s: 6
t: 1 hour 45 minutes
> Another recipe from Ilse Kook. A hearty, flavour-packed dish perfect for a family feast or festive table!. This is a go to in our house, and the left-overs the next day is incredible… if there are left-overs.
- 1 whole chicken, cut into 8 portions
- ¾ tsp dried chilli flakes
- 100 ml olive oil
- 1 red onion, cut into thin wedges
- 4 tomatoes, thickly sliced
- 450 g cherry tomatoes
- ¼ cup fresh basil and oregano (plus extra for serving)
- 2 tbsp thyme leaves
- 125 ml dry white wine
- 1 bulb of garlic, halved horizontally
- 5 slices rolled pancetta
- 120 g large green olives
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste
. Preheat the oven to 180°C.
. Heat 2 tbsp olive oil in an ovenproof roasting dish. Brown the chicken pieces on all sides, then remove and set aside.
. In the same dish, sauté the onion gently until soft. Add the chilli flakes and stir through. Place half of the sliced tomatoes and half of the cherry tomatoes over the sautéed onions.
. Spread evenly, then return the chicken pieces to the dish, placing them on top of the tomatoes. Scatter the remaining tomatoes, basil, oregano, and thyme over the chicken. Season with salt and pepper. Drizzle over the wine and the remaining olive oil. Tuck the halved garlic bulb among the chicken pieces.
. Roast uncovered for 1 to 1½ hours, until the chicken is golden brown and the tomato mixture is rich and jammy. Add the olives to the dish.
. Lay the pancetta slices on top of the chicken and grill under the oven’s broiler for 4–5 minutes, or until crispy. Crumble the crisped pancetta over the dish and scatter with the remaining fresh herbs before serving.

@ Heirloom Tomato and Whipped Feta Tart | tv | 28
s: 4
t: 1 hour
> This delicious tart is easy to make and super-tasty, This recipe is an adaptation to one of the tarts we filmed for the Plaaskombuis series, which I adapted a little.
- 250g all-butter puff pastry
- 250g mixed heirloom baby tomatoes, halved
- 100g feta cheese, whipped
- Zest of 1 lemon
- Small bunch of chives, chopped
- Milk for glazing
. Preheat oven to 210°C. Line a 26cm square roaster with pastry, folding the edges over slightly.
. Combine lemon zest with whipped feta, spread onto the pastry base, and scatter the tomatoes on top.
. Brush edges of pastry with milk, fold over, and brush again. Bake for 20–30 minutes until golden brown.
. Allow to cool slightly, garnish with chives, drizzle with olive oil, and season with salt and pepper.

@ Andrea Burgner’s Cilbir | tv | 29
s: 2
t: 20 min
> Having met and worked with Andrea Burgner, owner of The Leopard in Johannesburg, was one of the coolest experiences of my food journey. Andrea started out as a home cook with a knack for great combo’s. This Turkish Style breakfast is a go-to-favourite.
- 2 garlic cloves, very finely slivered
- 2½ cups plain Greek-style yoghurt (or use the labneh recipe from page 46)
- 2–3 T white wine vinegar
- 4 very fresh free-range eggs
- 2 T butter
- A few fresh or dried sage
- 1 T smoked Spanish paprika
- Sea salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste
- Bread or Pita, for serving
. Stir the garlic into the yoghurt (or labneh) and spread evenly over two serving plates to form little beds for the eggs.
. Bring a small saucepan of water to the boil, add the vinegar, and gently lower the first egg into the water. (Creating a whirlpool is optional, though not essential.) Poach each egg for about 3 minutes for a soft yolk. Keep the poached eggs warm under a lid or on a warm plate.
. While the eggs are cooking, melt the butter in a pan. Stir in the sage (if using fresh, fry until crisp) or parsley. Add the paprika and remove from the heat.
. Remove the eggs with a slotted spoon and place them gently onto the yoghurt. Spoon the paprika butter over the eggs, season with sea salt and black pepper, and serve immediately with lots of warm bread.
! Oil a piece of clingwrap in a cup, add the egg and tie up. Poach in the plastic and remove he egg when cooked.

@ Easy Milktart | tv | 30
s: 10-12
t: 1 hour
> In the show Plaaskombuis, I had the distinct pleasure to travel with Elma Postma and a small team and direct the show. The premise was that we would go to farm kitchen’s across South Africa and cook with the men and woman of the farming community in their kitchens. This recipe is from Owen & Louise McGregor, from the farm Bagdad’s Cafe where they serve this easy milktart in the farm stall.
- 1 packet (200g) Tennis biscuits
- 100 g butter, melted
- 1 liter milk
- 5 ml vanilla essence
- 2 ml almond essence
- 30 ml butter or margarine
- 250 ml sugar
- 50 ml cornstarch
- 20 ml custard powder
- 2 eggs
# For the Crust
. Grease a medium-sized tart dish. Crush the Tennis biscuits finely and mix them with the melted butter or margarine.
. Press the mixture firmly into the prepared tart dish, using the back of a spoon to push the crumbs against the bottom and sides. Refrigerate until needed.
# For the Filling
. Heat 750 ml (3 cups) of the milk with the vanilla and almond essence in a saucepan over medium heat until just below boiling point. Add the butter or margarine and allow it to melt in the milk.
. In a separate bowl, mix the sugar, cornstarch, and custard powder with the remaining 250 ml (1 cup) of milk. Add the eggs and whisk well.
. Slowly add a little of the warm milk mixture to the egg mixture, whisking continuously to temper the eggs. Pour the tempered egg mixture in a thin stream back into the saucepan, stirring constantly.
. Continue stirring over medium heat until the mixture thickens and comes to a boil.
. Pour the filling into the prepared crust and smooth it out evenly. Lightly sprinkle ground cinnamon over the top and allow the tart to cool to room temperature before serving.
